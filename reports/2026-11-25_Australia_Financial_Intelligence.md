## Executive Summary

1. **Australia's inflation and unemployment both surged within the same week, the first simultaneous stagflation-shaped signal of this cycle.** The ABS's October Labour Force survey (published 19 November) showed unemployment jumping to 4.9% — the highest since October 2021, with employment falling sharply — while the October monthly CPI (published 25 November, today) showed headline inflation accelerating to 4.9% year-on-year (from 4.3% in September) and trimmed mean to 3.8% (from 3.6%), both beating consensus. A rising jobless rate and accelerating inflation moving together, days before the RBA's 8 December decision and just ahead of 2 December Q3 GDP, materially complicates the "hold in December" view that had hardened after the hawkish 3 November minutes (reported last week) and a 17 November CommBank note explicitly calling December "not a live meeting." Market reaction to the 19 November jobs print reportedly included AUD/USD weakness and a pull-forward of rate-cut pricing; this report could not yet confirm the market's reaction to today's CPI print, which arrived only hours before this report's research cutoff.
2. **Northern Star Resources' 18 November AGM delivered a first strike on its remuneration report (61.51% in favour, 38.49% against) at the same meeting that completed its chair succession** — Michael Chaney stepped down and deputy chair Michael Ashforth succeeds him, while new CEO Suresh Vadnagra's FY27 incentive packages passed with comparatively soft support (~79–80%). Activist investor Elliott Investment Management reportedly said its push for board renewal and a strategic review has "not diminished" despite the leadership change. This is the second shareholder-pay rebuke at a major ASX company in five months, after Macquarie Group's requisitioned constitutional-amendment vote in July.
3. **ASIC used its 17–18 November Annual Forum to confirm 2027 enforcement priorities (26-286MR) explicitly naming private credit and managed investment scheme disclosure, governance, conflicts and valuation practices alongside a new, cost-of-living-driven focus on bank, insurer and superannuation-trustee misconduct, and a new AI/automated-decision-systems consumer-harm priority** — continuing, rather than closing, the campaign this report has tracked since ASIC's Bathla Group/Angas Securities actions two weeks ago. The interim stop order against the two Angas Securities funds is approaching its 21-day deadline (issued ~9 November) with no resolution confirmed inside this window.
4. **NextDC's 20 November AGM formally confirmed the FY27 capital expenditure guidance cut flagged on 21 October — to A$4,500–5,000m, a 17–19% reduction from original guidance** — closing out a continuity item this report has tracked since the Goodman Group/Digital Realty stake-build two weeks ago; the two strategic shareholders reiterated via the chairman's address that they have no current takeover intention.
5. **Nvidia's first results under interim CEO Colette Kress (17 November, just ahead of this window but reported only now) beat expectations materially** — revenue of US$115.8bn (+103% y/y) and record Data Center revenue of US$107.6bn, with FY2028 growth guidance raised to ~75% from ~70% — a result that, if it holds into ASX trading sentiment this week, offsets some of last week's AI-leadership-risk concern for Macquarie/GIC's Theseus Infrastructure platform and ASX-listed data-centre names such as NextDC.

## Top Developments

### 1. Inflation and unemployment rise together: a stagflation-shaped signal ahead of the RBA's 8 December decision
**Fact**: the ABS published its October 2026 Labour Force survey on 19 November, showing the seasonally adjusted unemployment rate rising to 4.9% — up from 4.7% in September and the highest reading since October 2021 — with employment falling sharply (secondary sources report a fall in the order of 44,000, against market expectations of a modest gain) and roughly 18,000 more people unemployed; the trend unemployment rate rose to 4.8%; youth unemployment (15–24) rose to 11.3% from 11.1%; underemployment eased slightly to 6.2%. Secondary coverage reports AUD/USD weakening on the release and rates markets pulling forward expectations for an eventual RBA rate cut. Separately, the ABS published its October 2026 monthly CPI indicator on 25 November (today), showing headline annual inflation accelerating to 4.9% — up from 4.3% in September and the highest in three years — against consensus estimates clustered around 4.3–4.8% depending on the source. Trimmed mean (underlying) inflation rose to 3.8% from 3.6%. Housing was the largest contributor, up 6.4% over the year, with electricity up 25.2%. A 17 November CommBank research note (published before both prints) had judged the RBA's 8 December meeting "not a live meeting" and expected a hold, on the view that two consecutive hikes (including the 6-3 split vote on 3 November) had already done enough; that note also flagged October CPI (24/25 November) and Q3 GDP (2 December) as the two remaining data points that could change the calculus before the meeting.
**Analysis**: this is a materially different combination from anything this report has tracked across the current hiking cycle — until now, the RBA's dilemma has been framed as "inflation risk vs. a cooling but still-functioning labour market" (see last week's minutes coverage, where dissenters cited the unemployment rate as a reason to pause). A labour market that is now visibly weakening (4.9% unemployment, falling employment) at the same time inflation is accelerating (4.9% CPI, broadening into housing and energy) removes the clean trade-off the Board's hawkish 3 November reasoning depended on. It does not resolve which risk the RBA will weight more heavily, but it raises the stakes of the 8 December decision considerably beyond a routine "hold vs. hike" call, and reopens — rather than settles — the question markets had provisionally answered after the jobs data alone.
**Inference**: a CPI print this far above both the September trend and most analyst forecasts, arriving one week after a labour-market print that would normally argue for easing, plausibly reopens the case for the RBA weighing a hike more seriously than the post-jobs-data "hold consensus" implied — but this report cannot yet confirm post-CPI market pricing (futures, economist commentary) for a December hike or hold, since the data was released only hours before this report's research cutoff.

### 2. Northern Star's AGM: first strike on pay, chair succession completes, Elliott's governance pressure persists
**Fact**: Northern Star Resources held its AGM on 18 November 2026. The remuneration report received only 61.51% support (38.49% against) — a first strike under the two-strikes rule. All eleven other resolutions were carried, including: the re-election of directors Jeff Quartermaine (98.79%), Terry Bowen (98.81%), Mark Cutifani (98.85%) and Peter Rozenauers (98.84%), and Sally Langer's re-election at a comparatively softer 83.78%; new CEO Suresh Vadnagra's FY27 short-term incentive performance rights (110,350 rights) passed at 79.27% and his FY27 sign-on rights (194,457 rights) at 80.29%; a director fee-pool increase passed at 99.85%; and renewal of proportional takeover provisions passed at 99.95%. This follows the board and management changes flagged in prior reports: Vadnagra replaced long-serving CEO Stuart Tonkin on 5 October, following activist pressure from Elliott Investment Management. Per secondary coverage, Elliott (a stake reportedly exceeding A$1bn) responded to the chair transition by stating that the need for "substantial board enhancement" and a "comprehensive strategic review" has "not diminished," following the board's earlier rejection of its proposed slate of six new directors.
**Analysis**: a first strike, rather than outright rejection, signals meaningful but not majority shareholder discontent — most plausibly concentrated on the CEO's sign-on/incentive package given the comparatively soft 79–80% support on those specific resolutions, arriving in a CEO's first months and amid Elliott's known push for governance change. Read together with Macquarie Group's own July 2026 AGM, where shareholders rejected a requisitioned constitutional amendment (a different kind of governance friction, not a pay strike), this is the second instance this report has tracked this year of organised shareholder pushback at a major ASX company.

### 3. ASIC confirms 2027 enforcement priorities: private credit remains targeted as a cost-of-living-driven focus broadens, with AI as a new addition
**Fact**: ASIC's Annual Forum ran 17–18 November 2026 at ICC Sydney. Chair Sarah Court's 18 November address and media release 26-286MR ("ASIC to target cost of living and tech-related consumer harm in 2027") set out ASIC's 2027 enforcement priorities. Per secondary synthesis (a Mallesons insight and search-engine summarisation of the release; the primary release and PDF could not be directly fetched in this session — see Uncertainty), the priorities include: (i) misconduct by banks, insurers and superannuation trustees that compounds cost-of-living pressure, including failures to deliver promised pricing, discounts or entitlements; (ii) private credit and managed investment scheme disclosure, governance, conflicts-of-interest and valuation practices; (iii) predatory practices targeting consumers and small businesses in financial difficulty, including harmful debt collection; (iv) harmful financial advice and lead-generation models; (v) consumer harm from poorly implemented technology, including AI and automated decision-making systems — a new addition this year; and (vi) misconduct by auditors and other professional gatekeepers — six of nine named priorities identified in search results, with the remaining three not located. Per secondary reporting, Commissioner Simone Constant characterised November's private-credit surveillance findings (Report 846 — only 2 of 22 assessed funds passed the full economic benefit of fees through to investors, covered in last week's report) as close to the sector's "final warning" before a shift toward 2027 enforcement. A Clayton Utz insight (November 2026) separately notes ASIC expects to consult in early 2027 on extending Regulatory Guide 45 (currently covering retail unlisted mortgage schemes) to private credit funds more broadly. A Treasury consultation on enhanced private-credit/managed-investment-scheme registration and data-collection requirements, open since 23 September, closes 28 November 2026 — just after this window. On the specific Bathla Group/Angas Securities matter flagged in last week's report, this week's research found no confirmation of the Angas interim stop orders' resolution; Angas's capital raising remains targeted for resolution by 31 December 2026, per its executive chairman's earlier public comments.
**Analysis**: this converts last week's open question — whether ASIC's 2027 priorities would continue to single out private credit after the Bathla collapse — into a confirmed yes, while also broadening the regulator's stated focus toward bank/insurer/super-trustee cost-of-living misconduct and, notably, AI-related consumer harm. The AI priority is worth flagging against last week's PwC finding that banks are leaning on AI as a competitive differentiator: ASIC appears to be aiming its 2027 supervisory lens at the same adoption trend from the consumer-protection side just as it accelerates from the efficiency side. The cost-of-living framing also connects naturally to this week's inflation data (#1), giving ASIC a politically resonant frame for scrutinising pricing and fee practices across retail financial services in 2027.
**Uncertainty**: this report could not directly fetch asic.gov.au or its PDF mirror (download.asic.gov.au) in this session — two independent access attempts (direct URL and PDF mirror) both failed with DNS resolution errors — so the full list of nine priorities and their exact wording is not independently verified; six of nine are reported here via secondary synthesis, and the remaining three are an acknowledged gap rather than an assumed absence. Constant's "final warning" characterisation should be treated as reported paraphrase, not direct quotation.
**Watch next**: direct confirmation of the complete nine-priority list from ASIC's own release; ASIC's first concrete enforcement action (as distinct from an interim stop order) under the 2027 private-credit priority; resolution of the Angas Securities interim stop order (due around 30 November); the outcome of Treasury's 28 November private-credit registration consultation.

## Emerging Trends

- **Inflation and unemployment are now rising together for the first time this cycle**, directly complicating the RBA's policy calculus just as market pricing had begun to settle on an expected December hold following the jobs data alone. This is a genuinely new dynamic, not a continuation of prior weeks' "inflation vs. growth" framing, and deserves close tracking into the 8 December decision.
