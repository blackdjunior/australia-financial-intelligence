# Australia Financial Intelligence — Weekly Report
**Week of 19–25 November 2026**

## Executive Summary

1. **The ABS's October Labour Force data, released 19 November, blindsided the RBA's own hawkish narrative from just two weeks earlier**: unemployment jumped to 4.9% (highest since October 2021, vs. 4.7% consensus) as employment fell 43,500 against a forecast ~25,000 gain. This lands just 16 days after the RBA's 3 November minutes (published 17 Nov, covered last week) argued the bigger risk was leaving policy "insufficiently restrictive." AUD/USD slid below 0.6700 and markets reportedly brought forward RBA rate-cut expectations to as early as June 2027; 8 December is now widely expected to be a hold.
2. **ASIC used its 18 November Annual Forum to release its 2027 enforcement priorities**, headlined by bank/insurer/super-trustee misconduct that compounds cost-of-living pressure, predatory practices targeting financially distressed consumers, and — new this year — consumer harm from poorly implemented AI and automated decision systems. Commissioner Simone Constant reportedly characterised November's private-credit surveillance findings (Report 846, covered last week) as the sector's "final warning" before 2027 enforcement.
3. **NextDC's 20 November AGM reaffirmed, rather than deepened, October's ~18% FY27 capex guidance cut**, and reportedly upgraded revenue/EBITDA guidance the same morning — arriving in the same week as Nvidia's blowout Q3 FY2027 beat (US$115.8bn revenue, +103% YoY). Read together, AI-infrastructure demand looks intact; the constraint is increasingly physical (grid connection delays) rather than financial.
4. **Northern Star Resources' 18 November AGM resolved the chair succession (Michael Ashforth succeeds Michael Chaney) but not the underlying governance dispute**: Elliott Investment Management says its push for board renewal and a strategic review "has not diminished."
5. **This report could not corroborate last week's Korean-press claim that Hanwha is "ahead of" Wildcat Infrastructure in the Austal USA contest**, and separately found a likely date discrepancy in prior Korea Zinc/Ark Energy reporting — both flagged as open corrections rather than resolved silently.

## Top Developments

### 1. October jobs shock (unemployment 4.9%, -43,500) upends the RBA's hawkish-minutes narrative within a fortnight
**Fact**: the ABS released October Labour Force data on 19 November 2026: the seasonally adjusted unemployment rate rose to 4.9% (from 4.7% in September), the highest since October 2021 and above consensus (~4.7%). Employment fell by 43,500 (seasonally adjusted), against a forecast ~25,000 gain; the participation rate eased to 66.6%; youth unemployment rose to 11.3%. This arrives 16 days after the RBA's 3 November minutes (published 17 Nov, covered in last week's report) described the majority's view that the larger risk was leaving policy "insufficiently restrictive" and projected headline inflation above 3% until late 2027. AUD/USD fell below 0.6700 on the release, extending a slide that had already taken it near a multi-month low earlier in November; rates markets reportedly brought forward expectations for an RBA cut to as early as June 2027. The ASX 200 fell roughly 1.0% on the day, a move attributed primarily to miners rather than a broad rate-sensitive rally. CommBank economists are reported to now view the 8 December meeting as "not a live meeting," expecting a hold.
**Analysis**: this is the fastest and sharpest reversal of a stated RBA reaction-function narrative this report series has tracked — a single data print arriving barely two weeks after the Board's own minutes built a case for a durable, data-independent hawkish stance (a higher neutral rate). It does not overturn that argument on its own, but it meaningfully strengthens the position of the three dissenting board members (out of nine) who, per the same 3 November minutes, cited labour-market risk as their basis for opposing the hike.
**Uncertainty**: abs.gov.au could not be fetched directly in this research session (a recurring access limitation across this report series); the headline figures are cross-confirmed across at least four independent secondary aggregators quoting the same release, which this report treats as reliable despite the absence of a direct primary fetch. The AUD/USD sub-0.6700 print is reported by a single source without a precise timestamp.
**Watch next**: any RBA speech before 8 December reconciling the jobs miss with the 3 November minutes' hawkish framing; the November Labour Force survey, due after the December decision.

### 2. ASIC's 2027 enforcement priorities escalate private credit from surveillance to a stated "final warning"
**Fact**: ASIC Chair Sarah Court announced the regulator's 2027 enforcement priorities (26-286MR) at ASIC's Annual Forum on 18 November 2026. Per secondary summary, priorities include misconduct by banks, insurers and superannuation trustees that compounds cost-of-living pressure on consumers; predatory lending/sales practices targeting consumers and small businesses in financial difficulty; and, new this year, consumer harm from poorly implemented AI and automated decision-making systems. Commissioner Simone Constant reportedly described November's private-credit surveillance findings (Report 846 — only 2 of 22 assessed funds passed the full economic benefit of fees through to investors, covered in last week's report) as effectively the sector's "final warning" before a shift toward enforcement in 2027.
**Analysis**: read with last week's Bathla Group collapse and Angas Securities stop orders, this converts the private-credit story from a pattern of scattered interim stop orders into an explicitly stated regulatory escalation path. The new AI/automated-systems priority is also notable set against last week's PwC finding that banks are leaning on AI as a competitive differentiator — ASIC appears to be aiming its 2027 supervisory lens at the same trend from the consumer-protection side just as adoption accelerates from the efficiency side.
**Uncertainty**: asic.gov.au and the 26-286MR PDF could not be fetched directly in this session (same recurring access block affecting every report in this series); the full list of stated priorities beyond the three above was not independently confirmed, and Constant's "final warning" characterisation should be treated as reported paraphrase, not direct quotation.
**Watch next**: ASIC's first concrete enforcement action (as distinct from an interim stop order) under the 2027 private-credit priority; resolution of the Angas Securities interim stop orders, due to lapse around 30 November.

### 3. NextDC reaffirms, not deepens, its capex cut the same week Nvidia posts a blowout quarter
**Fact**: at NextDC's AGM on 20 November 2026, outgoing chairman Douglas Flynn (his 13th and final AGM) confirmed FY27 capital-expenditure guidance of A$4,500–5,000m — a reduction of roughly A$980m (17–19% like-for-like) from the original 27 August 2026 guidance, following grid-connection delays at the M3 Melbourne site (works not expected to resume before February 2027). The same AGM statement said the company had upgraded its FY27 net revenue and Underlying EBITDA guidance that same morning; the exact revised figures were not confirmed in this research session. FY26 net revenue (A$405.0m) and Underlying EBITDA (A$248.8m) had both exceeded prior guidance's top end. CEO Craig Scroggie has forgone FY27 short- and long-term incentive opportunities. This landed the same week as Nvidia's 17 November Q3 FY2027 results: revenue US$115.8bn (+103% YoY), Data Center segment US$107.6bn, EPS US$2.68 vs. US$2.48 expected, with Q4 guidance of US$130bn — a comfortable beat despite Jensen Huang's abrupt leadership exit (covered last week) and confirmation he will not be reappointed as CEO.
**Analysis**: this is a more precise read than last week's single "Nvidia leadership risk" framing. The capex cut itself is not new this week (announced 21 Oct); what is new is that the AGM did not deepen it and paired the reaffirmation with a claimed revenue/EBITDA upgrade — consistent with a company-specific execution delay (grid capacity) rather than a demand-side AI-capex pullback. Set against Nvidia's results, AI-infrastructure demand looks intact; the binding constraint this report is tracking looks increasingly physical and regulatory (grid connection) rather than financial or sentiment-driven.
**Uncertainty**: the claimed same-day revenue/EBITDA upgrade is sourced to a single secondary aggregator summarising the AGM statement; the revised figures should be verified against the ASX announcement directly before being treated as confirmed.
**Watch next**: the specific revised FY27 net revenue/EBITDA range once published; whether Goodman Group/Digital Realty's 9.9% stake develops into a formal proposal; any analyst read on whether NextDC's capex discipline is company-specific or an early signal of broader Australian data-centre capex moderation.

### 4. Northern Star's AGM resolves the chair succession; the Elliott governance dispute does not go away
**Fact**: at Northern Star Resources' AGM on 18 November 2026, Michael Chaney retired as chairman (as foreshadowed since June) and deputy chair Michael Ashforth succeeded him. The dispute over board composition and strategic direction looks likely to continue into 2027.
