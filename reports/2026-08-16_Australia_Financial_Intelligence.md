## Top Developments

### 1. RBA holds rates at 4.35%, keeps a hawkish door open
At its 11 August meeting, the RBA's Monetary Policy Board left the cash rate unchanged at 4.35%, the second straight hold. **Fact**: trimmed-mean inflation remains elevated and little changed since the March quarter; the Board statement said the impact of the Middle East conflict on inflation has so far been *less* than feared, but headline inflation is still too high. Governor Bullock said the Board "remains concerned" about the inflation outlook and needs "further progress" before it can be confident inflation will return to target under current settings. **Analysis**: this is a Board deliberately keeping optionality — not signalling imminent hikes, but not closing the door either, largely because the Hormuz-driven oil shock (see #3 below) is a live upside risk to imported inflation. **Inference**: absent a further oil-driven inflation surprise, the big four banks' current house view (no near-term move) looks like the base case; the next scheduled decision is 29 September 2026.
[RBA media release](https://www.rba.gov.au/media-releases/2026/mr-26-19.html) · [Domain coverage](https://www.domain.com.au/news/rba-august-2026-1541698/)

### 2. Bank reporting season: record CBA profit, resilient ANZ, but Westpac's mortgage warning spooks the sector
**Fact**: CBA's FY26 (year to 30 June) results, released 12 August, showed cash EPS up 7% to 656.9c, ROE up 50bp to 14.0%, and a final dividend of $2.70 (full-year $5.05) — a record profit on the back of 7% loan growth and steady margins, partly offset by 6% opex growth. ANZ's Q3 (to 30 June) trading update on 13 August showed cash profit of $1.90bn, up ~5% excluding a NZ court-related provision, CET1 up to 12.51%, and housing lending returning to system growth. Westpac's Q3 update showed underlying profit of $1.8bn (+2% on the 1H26 quarterly average) but flagged mortgage applications down roughly 20% quarter-on-quarter. **Analysis**: the market read the Westpac mortgage data as a leading indicator for the whole sector — net interest margin tailwinds from prior cash-rate settings are being competed away faster than expected in home lending and deposits, and slower mortgage volumes point to softer credit growth into 2027. That reading, combined with weaker commodity prices, sent the ASX 200 down 1.6% for the week (financials -0.12%, materials -2.58%) to 9,115.2 — its worst week since April. **Fact/Analysis separation**: the profit numbers are fact; the "property boom is fading" framing used by several outlets is analyst interpretation, not yet a confirmed trend.
[CBA FY26 results](https://www.commbank.com.au/content/dam/commbank-assets/investors/2026/CBA-2026-Full-Year-Results-Profit-Announcement.pdf) · [ANZ Q3 update](https://www.anz.com.au/newsroom/media/2026/august/2026-thirdquarter-trading-update/) · [Westpac mortgage warning coverage](https://kalkine.com.au/news/general-news/commonwealth-bank-asxcba-falls-ahead-of-results-as-westpacs-mortgage-warning-sends-a-chill-through-the-banks) · [Weekly market wrap](https://www.marketindex.com.au/news/asx-200-live-today-friday-14th-august)

### 3. Macquarie, GIC and Anthropic launch "Theseus Infrastructure"
**Fact**: on 10 August, Anthropic, Macquarie Asset Management and GIC announced a strategic partnership to build "Theseus Infrastructure," a platform to develop, own and lease AI data-centre capacity to Anthropic under long-term agreements, with Macquarie-managed funds and GIC jointly owning the platform and funding the majority of project equity. Initial focus is the US. **Analysis**: this is a significant, direct example of an Australian-headquartered asset manager underwriting AI infrastructure at scale, alongside a major Asian sovereign-adjacent investor (GIC, Singapore) — it signals that global institutional capital, including capital Macquarie sources and manages, is treating AI compute infrastructure as a core private-markets asset class, not a speculative side bet. **What to watch**: whether this becomes a template Macquarie replicates with other hyperscalers/AI labs, and whether Korean pension/sovereign capital (which has separately been active in Australian infrastructure and energy transition assets — see Korea Angle) seeks co-investment exposure to similar platforms.
[Macquarie/GIC/Anthropic announcement](https://www.macquarie.com/au/en/about/news/2026/anthropic-mam-gic-data-centre-infrastructure-partnership.html)

### 4. Strait of Hormuz standoff keeps driving global risk and oil prices
**Fact**: the IEA this week cut its 2026 oil demand forecast, citing the Hormuz disruption as the largest supply shock in the history of the global oil market; shipping data show only 8–15 vessel transits a day through the Strait versus ~130 pre-conflict. Oil (WTI) rose roughly 5% mid-week to around $82/barrel on doubts that the US and Iran will reach a deal to restore shipping; Iran's Foreign Minister said the Strait would not reopen until sanctions relief and reparations conditions are met. **Analysis**: this is the dominant macro backdrop for the week — it is feeding directly into the RBA's inflation caution (imported energy costs), into ASX weakness (risk-off, commodity volatility), and into AUD/USD forecasts, which see the RBA's "restraint" bias as a key 2026 driver alongside Fed policy. **Uncertainty**: the negotiation path is genuinely unresolved; forecasts assuming either a quick resolution or a prolonged shock are both currently defensible, and this report does not take a view on the outcome.

## Markets

- **Equities**: ASX 200 closed the week at 9,115.2, down 0.8% on Friday and 1.6% for the week — the worst weekly performance since April. All Ordinaries fell 1.4% for the week to 9,313.2. Materials led losses (-2.58%) as gold snapped a nine-session winning streak (still +9.4% for the month); financials were comparatively resilient (-0.12%) despite the bank-earnings volatility; technology stocks continued to outperform on rotation.
- **FX**: AUD/USD forecasts diverge — near-term seasonality is historically weak for AUD in August, but medium-term views lean toward AUD strength into year-end on the view that the RBA's restraint bias contrasts with expected Fed easing pressure. The Hormuz-driven oil shock is a live wildcard for this thesis in either direction.
- **Rates**: RBA on hold at 4.35% (see Top Developments). Domestic bond issuance remains strong — Australian corporate bond issuance was up 42% in H1 2026 to A$191.7bn, with superannuation funds, banks and foreign investors all cited as active buyers, drawn to Australia's stable regulatory environment and relatively attractive yields even at current rate levels.
- **Credit**: no major spread-widening events reported this week; the broader 2026 story remains one of strong investor demand for Australian credit (AUD market now ranks fourth globally by issuance behind EUR, USD and CAD).

## Regulation & Policy

- **ASIC** reported it took 150 enforcement actions in FY25/26, removing or restricting 114 individuals/businesses from providing financial or credit services, disqualifying 36 individuals from managing corporations, and banning 15 advisers linked to the Shield and First Guardian collapsed-fund matters. ASIC's stated 2026 priorities add misleading pricing, private credit, insurance complaints and financial reporting as elevated focus areas, alongside a stated intent to keep increasing litigation and criminal referrals.
- **APRA/ASIC** jointly continued streamlining the Financial Accountability Regime (FAR) — raising materiality thresholds for notifying changes in accountability and removing some reporting requirements — following a 16 June announcement and 16 July updated Statements of Intent. This is background rather than this-week news but remains a live compliance-burden theme for regulated institutions.
- **AI governance**: firms had a 1 July 2026 deadline to make AI-vendor contracts CPS 230-compliant (now passed); Privacy Act amendments imposing new transparency obligations on automated decision-making are due later in 2026 and remain a forward compliance item for any institution scaling AI in customer-facing or credit-decisioning processes.

## Emerging Trends

- **Institutional capital moving directly into AI infrastructure.** The Macquarie/GIC/Anthropic data-centre platform is the clearest Australian-linked example yet of asset managers treating AI compute as core private-markets infrastructure rather than a venture-style bet — a trend worth tracking across other Australian infrastructure managers (IFM, QIC, etc.) in coming months.
- **Bank margin compression resurfacing as a theme.** Westpac's mortgage-application drop and the market's sharp reaction suggest investors are newly sensitive to competitive pressure on net interest margins after several quarters of relative calm — this is an emerging, not yet confirmed, trend and bears watching in NAB's upcoming full-year result and in October housing-finance data.
- **Private credit under increased regulatory scrutiny.** ASIC naming private credit as a new 2026 enforcement priority, layered on its Shield/First Guardian enforcement actions, points to tightening oversight of a fast-growing, less-transparent segment of Australian wealth products.
- **Capital markets re-opening.** FY26 ASX IPO capital raised ($5.6bn) and follow-on capital ($37.8bn, +20% YoY) both came in above five-year averages, with the FY27 pipeline described as the strongest in four years — a potential tailwind for investment banking and capital-markets revenue into H2 2026 if the Hormuz situation doesn't derail sentiment.
