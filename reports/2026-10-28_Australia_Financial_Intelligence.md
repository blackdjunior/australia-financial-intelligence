## Top Developments

### 1. Hot September-quarter CPI reopens RBA hike risk for 3 November
**Fact**: The ABS's September-quarter CPI, released 28 October 2026, showed headline inflation at 4.3% y/y (up from 4.0% in the 12 months to August) and 1.3% q/q (above the ~1.2% consensus forecast, up from 0.6% the prior quarter). The RBA's preferred trimmed-mean measure rose 1.0% q/q, above the RBA's own 0.8% forecast and above bank forecasts clustered at 0.9–1.0%. Annual trimmed mean is given as 3.6% on the ABS's own release (up from a revised 3.5%); some wire reports (InvestingLive) instead cite 3.7% y/y — this report flags rather than resolves the discrepancy, treating the ABS's own figure as the stronger source. Fuel/automotive costs, which are excluded from the trimmed mean, were the main driver of the gap between the hot headline number and the smaller (but still above-forecast) core read. The RBA cash rate is 4.60%, following a unanimous 9-0 hike on 29 September 2026 (the fourth hike of 2026, from 4.35%) — the highest level since 2011. The next decision is 3 November 2026 (2:30pm AEDT), alongside the quarterly Statement on Monetary Policy.
**Analysis**: this is a sharp reversal, within the same reporting cycle, of the dovish shift this project's own 21 October report identified — a weak September labour print (released 15 Oct) had collapsed November-hike pricing toward a hold. A hot CPI print of this magnitude, specifically a trimmed mean above the RBA's own forecast, materially reopens the case for a November hike, which would be the RBA's fifth of 2026.
**Inference**: market commentary post-release (MNI, "market wary the RBA may disappoint again") suggests hike-probability pricing has moved up again, but this report could not obtain a clean, dated post-CPI hike-probability figure and does not state one as fact.
**Uncertainty**: this report was researched the same day as the CPI release; post-CPI ASX 200, AUD/USD and bond-market reaction data were not reliably available at research time (see Markets section) and are not reported as fact.
**Watch next**: the 3 November RBA decision itself; whether the trimmed-mean/headline discrepancy narrows in subsequent data; any RBA commentary explicitly weighing this print against the softer labour data from two weeks earlier.

### 2. Strait of Hormuz tanker-attack tempo hits a new monthly record; motive narratives grow more contested
**Fact**: Reuters reported (23 Oct) that, per the Washington Institute for Near East Policy's shipping-incident tracker, October 2026 saw 28 tanker/ship attacks in the Strait of Hormuz — the highest monthly count of the conflict (113 attacks since it began around 28 February 2026). The week of 5–11 October separately recorded the single highest weekly attack count (around a dozen ships hit). A separate Reuters review of IMO incident data and MarineTraffic tracking found more than 30 VLCCs damaged since the conflict began, 11 of them in October alone. Further reported incidents this week: a tanker struck by an unidentified projectile transiting the Strait (22 Oct, via UKMTO-linked reporting); a supertanker reportedly struck while exiting the Strait (24 Oct, Shafaq News, Tehran-sourced, not independently corroborated); an Aframax tanker hit off Oman with a fire extinguished and crew reported safe (21 Oct, secondary source, not independently verified). Reuters separately reported that two senior Iranian officials, speaking anonymously, said Tehran is deliberately sustaining the attack tempo to keep oil prices elevated and influence the US midterm elections; Donald Trump has separately accused Iran (per reporting relayed via straits.live, citing the Jerusalem Post) of fabricating Hormuz attacks for the same timing reason.
**Analysis**: this is a direct continuation of the escalation this project has tracked across multiple reports (described as reaching "wartime high" tempo in early October); the new data point is that an independent tracker now confirms a monthly record, not just anecdotal reports of individual strikes.
**Inference**: the emergence of two mutually exclusive, politically-motivated claims about the authenticity/purpose of the attacks — one alleging deliberate Iranian price manipulation, the other alleging US-side fabrication — suggests the information environment around this conflict is degrading in reliability just as it becomes more materially relevant to Australian imported-inflation risk; this report treats both claims as unverified assertions, not fact.
**Uncertainty**: this report could not obtain a reliably-dated current Brent/WTI price for the 22–28 October window (see Markets section) and does not state one as fact.
**Watch next**: independent corroboration of the 22 and 24 October incidents; any oil-price data confirming the record attack tempo's price impact; further Reuters/Bloomberg reporting on the competing motive claims.

### 4. Korea-relevant ATRC steel-case developments: Dongkuk cleared of dumping; Investigation 688's POSCO-exclusion duties still await a ministerial decision
**Fact**: via Korean-language search, this report surfaced a partial final ruling by the Australian Trade Remedies Commission (Notice 2026/126, dated 18 September 2026) that terminated Investigation No. 706 (galvanised/zinc-coated steel from Korea and Vietnam) as it applies to Dongkuk Steel Mill, after finding no dumping on its exports — a fact not previously surfaced by this project. This is separate from, and earlier than, POSCO's clearance in the distinct Investigation No. 688 (plate steel), covered in last week's report. A Commission file note shows staff met with POSCO on 10 September 2026 to discuss product-scope/injury complexity in Investigation 706, which otherwise remains on its extended timeline (Statement of Essential Facts due by 5 March 2027, final report by 19 April 2027, per Notice 2026/141). Separately, Investigation No. 688's final report (26 August 2026) recommending duties on Chinese and other Korean plate-steel exporters while excluding POSCO still awaits a ministerial decision; none had been reported as of 28 October.
**Analysis**: together, these show the ATRC steadily clearing individual named Korean respondents (Dongkuk, POSCO) from dumping findings even as the broader 706 case timeline extends — a mixed but net-favourable pattern for the two largest named Korean exporters in these proceedings.
**Uncertainty**: the 18 September Dongkuk ruling is sourced to the Commission's own notice number as relayed via Korean-language secondary reporting; this report has not independently retrieved and read the notice's full text and flags the claim as well-sourced but not independently verified against the primary document.
**Watch next**: the ministerial decision on Investigation 688's recommended duties; whether Investigation 706's now-twice-extended timeline holds to the 5 March 2027 SEF date.

## Emerging Trends

- **The RBA's rate path is proving highly volatile week-to-week within a single reporting cycle.** A weak labour print (mid-October) collapsed November-hike pricing toward a hold; a hot CPI print two weeks later (28 October) appears to have reopened it. This is now the second consecutive report to catch and correct a sourcing issue of this kind (last week flagged, but did not fully resolve, the same underlying claim) — the pattern itself, not just the individual claim, is worth naming as a recurring risk in how this project verifies claims from secondary and lower-tier outlets.
