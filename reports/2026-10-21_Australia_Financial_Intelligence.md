## Top Developments

### 1. NextDC's share price collapses on a Melbourne grid-power delay, not a demand problem
**Fact**: On 21 October, NextDC (ASX: NXT) shares fell as much as 40–45% intraday to an approximate eight-/nine-year low (reports differ: ~$5.10–$5.48 range), after the company cut FY27 guidance. The trigger is a power-delivery delay at NextDC's M3 Melbourne data centre: electricity network operator Jemena suspended the grid-upgrade works that were to supply M3 after community opposition, and does not intend to resume work before February 2027, pushing the next increase in grid supply to M3 out to FY28. NextDC removed the affected M3 contracts (reported ~$140m in both net revenue and underlying EBITDA) from FY27 guidance, cutting net-revenue guidance from a previously reported $615–640m range down to $475–500m. The company says the underlying contracts remain in place — it is assessing service-level credits, termination rights and possible contractual relief rather than treating the contracts as lost — but is reviewing its capex programme, cutting planned FY27 capex by roughly $750m (to a new $4.5–5.0bn range) and placing all new developments and land acquisitions on hold pending the review.
**Analysis**: this is a different failure mode from Firmus Grid's withdrawn IPO (a capital-markets/demand problem) but the same underlying theme: Australian AI-infrastructure build-out is colliding with real-world delivery constraints — in Firmus's case investor scepticism about the gap between pipeline and built capacity, in NextDC's case a literal inability to get grid power connected on schedule because of community/planning opposition. Two of the sector's most prominent ASX-exposed names have now both been repriced sharply within a two-week window for related-but-distinct reasons.
**Inference**: if grid-connection delays of this kind are a systemic issue for data-centre developers generally (rather than NextDC/Jemena-specific), this is a new and under-priced risk factor for the broader Australian AI-infrastructure investment thesis — distinct from, and arguably more structural than, the valuation/demand risk that sank Firmus's IPO. This report has not yet found evidence either confirming or ruling out that this is a sector-wide pattern.
**Uncertainty**: this report is being researched and published on the same day as the share-price move; the final closing price, the ultimate market-cap impact, and any further company or regulatory response had not fully crystallised at the time of writing. Figures above should be treated as intraday/early reporting, not a final settled record.
**Watch next**: NextDC's final FY27 guidance detail and analyst reaction; whether other ASX-listed data-centre/AI-infrastructure names (Firmus, Firmus-linked Maas Group, or others) face similar grid-connection scrutiny; any statement from Jemena or the Victorian government on the community-opposition dispute.

### 2. Firmus Grid's aftermath: Nvidia explores a direct investment, ASIC starts gathering information
**Fact**: per The Information (reported via Bloomberg, 20 October), Nvidia is in discussions to invest "hundreds of millions of dollars" directly in Firmus Grid after the company's early-October IPO withdrawal, which sources frame as a signal Nvidia intends to keep backing debt-laden "neocloud" AI-infrastructure providers more broadly. Separately, also reported 20 October (Bloomberg, citing testimony at a parliamentary committee hearing in Sydney), ASIC's head of enforcement said the regulator is gathering information about the failed IPO, while stating no formal investigation has yet been opened.

**Uncertainty**: neither the Nvidia investment nor the scope/outcome of ASIC's information-gathering is confirmed or finalised; this report treats both as reported-but-unresolved.
**Watch next**: confirmation (or denial) of Nvidia's participation and its sizing; whether ASIC's information-gathering escalates to a formal investigation; the overall private-raise sizing and whether it implies a valuation materially below the withdrawn IPO's ~A$43.7bn mark.

### 3. Austal USA sale: Hanwha's due diligence extended, deal moving toward a binding proposal
**Fact**: in a 14 October ASX announcement, Austal Limited extended the due diligence period granted to Hanwha Defense USA, Inc. Austal stated the major/substantive data-room issues have been resolved, with only a tail of lower-priority data requests still open; that commercial negotiations between Hanwha and Austal have now begun on transaction details; and that Austal is obtaining tax advice on a potential deal. Austal shares reportedly rose ~10–11% on the announcement. Hanwha's indicative, non-binding offer remains in the US$1.05–1.2bn range (enterprise value, cash/debt-free basis); Wildcat Infrastructure's rival non-binding proposal (up to US$1.25–1.35bn) remains outstanding and under parallel consideration by Austal's board. No binding offer from either party, and no CFIUS determination, had been reported as of this week.
**Analysis**: the move from "resolving data-room issues" to "commercial negotiations under way" is a meaningful step toward an eventual binding Hanwha proposal, and is consistent with the Korean-language reporting cited in last week's report that Australia's government does not intend to intervene in the contest on political grounds. It does not resolve the underlying valuation gap with Wildcat's higher indicative offer, and Austal's board has said any binding proposal will be assessed on its merits, including shareholder interests.
**What should we watch next**: whether Hanwha converts to a binding offer in the coming weeks; whether Wildcat escalates into a formal competing bid; any CFIUS or other US regulatory development, given Austal USA's status as a major US government contractor.
**Korea relevance**: this is the week's most concrete, dated Korea-Australia-adjacent corporate development (Hanwha being a Korean conglomerate), even though the underlying transaction is a US-asset sale by an Australian-listed parent rather than a direct Korea-Australia capital flow.
