## Top Developments

### 1. NextDC's share price collapses on a Melbourne grid-power delay, not a demand problem
**Fact**: On 21 October, NextDC shares fell as much as 40–45% intraday to an approximate eight-/nine-year low (reports differ on the exact low, roughly $5.10–$5.48), after the company cut FY27 guidance. The trigger: Jemena suspended the grid-upgrade works that were to supply NextDC's M3 Melbourne data centre, after community opposition, and does not intend to resume work before February 2027, pushing the next increase in grid supply to M3 out to FY28. NextDC removed the affected M3 contracts (reported ~$140m in both net revenue and underlying EBITDA) from FY27 guidance, cutting net-revenue guidance from a previously reported $615–640m range to $475–500m. The company says the underlying contracts remain in place — it is assessing service-level credits, termination rights and possible contractual relief rather than treating them as lost — but is cutting planned FY27 capex by roughly $750m (to a new $4.5–5.0bn range) and pausing all new developments and land acquisitions pending review.
**Analysis**: this is a different failure mode from Firmus Grid's withdrawn IPO (a capital-markets/demand problem) but connects to the same underlying theme this project has tracked since August: Australian AI-infrastructure build-out colliding with real-world delivery constraints — in Firmus's case investor scepticism about pipeline vs. built capacity, in NextDC's case an inability to get grid power connected on schedule because of community/planning opposition. Two of the sector's most prominent ASX-exposed names have now been repriced sharply within a two-week window for related-but-distinct reasons.
**Inference**: if grid-connection delays of this kind are a systemic issue for data-centre developers generally (rather than NextDC/Jemena-specific), this is a new and under-priced risk factor for the broader Australian AI-infrastructure investment thesis — distinct from, and arguably more structural than, the valuation/demand risk that sank Firmus's IPO. This report has not found evidence either confirming or ruling out a sector-wide pattern.
**Uncertainty**: this report was researched the same day as the share-price move; the final closing price and ultimate market-cap impact had not fully crystallised at the time of writing. Figures above should be treated as intraday/early reporting.
**Watch next**: NextDC's final FY27 guidance detail and analyst reaction; whether other ASX-listed data-centre/AI-infrastructure names face similar grid-connection scrutiny; any statement from Jemena or the Victorian government.

### 2. Weak September jobs data collapses November rate-hike pricing; banks stage sharpest rally in months
**Fact**: The ABS's September labour force survey, released 15 October, showed employment fell by 39,300 (consensus expected a ~20,000 gain) and unemployment rose to 4.7% (a five-year high), against an expected 4.6%. The ABS urged caution interpreting the monthly figures, citing recent survey methodology changes. AUD/USD fell ~0.3% to around $0.6953 on the release. Market pricing for a hike at the RBA's 3–4 November meeting fell sharply: one Reuters-sourced report put the implied probability of a hold at ~90% (up from ~66% pre-release); InvestingLive separately reported November hike pricing falling to ~15% from just under 50% earlier in the month. **This report flags rather than resolves the discrepancy** between these two estimates, both pointing the same direction. CBA shares rose ~3% intraday with no company-specific news; other major banks also rallied, and multiple outlets described the session as the banks' best since February.
**Analysis**: this is a near-complete reversal, within two days, of the hawkish narrative set by the RBA's own 13 October minutes (covered last week), which had flagged a possibly-higher neutral rate and left the door open to further hikes. It also resolves — in the bullish direction — the bank-stock whipsaw flagged last week, though for a different reason than the housing-loan-application concerns cited by sell-side analysts the prior week: this is a rates-relief rally, not evidence the underlying mortgage-volume slowdown has reversed.
**Inference**: if the labour-market softening proves durable rather than a one-month survey artefact, it strengthens the case for an RBA hold at 4.60% in November and makes the 28 October CPI print the dominant remaining swing factor.
**Watch next**: the 28 October CPI print; whether October labour data (due mid-November, after the RBA meeting) corroborates or reverses the September weakness.
