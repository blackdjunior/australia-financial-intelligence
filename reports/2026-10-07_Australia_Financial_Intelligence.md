## Executive Summary

The Hanwha-vs-Wildcat Infrastructure bidding contest for Austal USA remains open and unresolved, with no binding deal reported.

## Top Developments

### 1. Metrics Credit Partners freezes over A$9bn after KPMG audit dispute; Bloomberg reports an ASIC investigation
**Fact**: On 28 September, Metrics Credit Partners — one of Australia's largest private-credit managers — placed three ASX-listed funds (Metrics Master Income Trust/MXT, Metrics Income Opportunities Trust/MOT, Metrics Real Estate Multi-Strategy Fund/MRE) into a trading halt after auditor KPMG reached different valuation and scenario-likelihood judgements than Metrics had used in its preliminary FY26 accounts, particularly on unlisted commercial-real-estate equity. Net tangible asset backing was cut by roughly 12% at MRE and smaller amounts at MOT/MXT, with the combined writedown across the three listed trusts reported at approximately A$168–170m. The halt and a related suspension from quotation continued through 30 September; Metrics also suspended distribution reinvestment plans for MXT and MOT from 30 September (distributions now paid in cash). On the same date, Metrics froze withdrawals on two large unlisted wholesale vehicles — the ~A$6bn Metrics Wholesale Investment Trust and the ~A$5.4bn MCP Real Estate Debt Fund — after KPMG said it would not sign off on their accounts by the 30 September statutory deadline either, for a 90-day period. Separately, Bloomberg reported that ASIC has opened a focused investigation into Metrics over loan valuations and governance as part of a broader, multi-year private-markets review (Metrics' AUM is reported variously as ~A$20bn and ~A$30bn across sources); Metrics issued a media statement saying it is "not aware of any ASIC investigation related to specific practices or transactions" in its business, while ASIC confirmed it is increasing surveillance of private-credit fund valuation and governance practices without commenting on Metrics by name.
**Analysis**: this is a step change from the Remara/Bathla stress covered in the 30 September report. Remara was a retail mis-selling action against three small products; Metrics is a flagship, ASX-listed manager freezing over A$9bn across five vehicles on its own auditor's objection to its valuations — a governance and credibility event for the sector's largest players, not just its fringes. It lands only three weeks after ASIC Commissioner Simone Constant told the private-credit industry (22 September) to "prepare for enforcement action" because the regulator is "well beyond warnings."
**Inference**: a listed fund freezing on an auditor's valuation objection — rather than on a borrower default — suggests the immediate issue is governance/disclosure rather than (yet) a confirmed credit-loss event; whether it proves to be the former or evolves into the latter is unresolved and this report takes no view. This is inference, not a confirmed fact.
**Watch next**: whether KPMG ultimately signs the FY26 accounts and on what terms; whether ASIC confirms a formal Metrics investigation; whether other large private-credit managers face comparable audit disputes; and whether superannuation funds with Metrics exposure disclose write-downs.

### 2. RBA's October Financial Stability Review calls the system resilient — published days before Metrics unravelled
**Fact**: On 1 October, the RBA released its half-yearly Financial Stability Review. Primary conclusions: the Australian financial system "has a good degree of resilience" with "no room for complacency"; most mortgaged households have positive equity and savings buffers; banks are "well capitalised" and able to keep lending through a downturn; and non-bank lender (private-credit) risk is described as "contained by their relatively small size" relative to the broader system, though data gaps remain in parts of non-bank lending. The Review separately warned that risks are "increasingly coming from abroad" (elevated sovereign debt, high asset prices, leverage), that global private-credit funds exposed to AI-sensitive sectors have in some cases had to limit investor redemptions, and specifically that "the boom in long-term debt financing to support AI investments is becoming more opaque and more vulnerable to profits falling short of expectations," alongside a more complex cyber-threat landscape driven by frontier AI capability.
**Analysis**: the FSR's sanguine framing of private-credit risk sits uncomfortably next to the Metrics freeze that broke the same week (#1) — not a contradiction (Metrics is, so far, a governance/valuation dispute rather than a demonstrated credit loss, and private credit remains small relative to bank balance sheets), but a reminder that "contained by size" is a system-level claim that does not rule out individual manager-level shocks with reputational spillover. This is also the RBA's own confirmation of a theme ASIC and APRA have separately escalated warnings about since mid-2026 — AI-linked leverage and cyber risk are now flagged across all three of Australia's main financial regulators/the central bank, each from a different angle.
**Watch next**: whether the RBA's November Statement on Monetary Policy or minutes revisit non-bank lending risk in light of Metrics; whether APRA follows with commentary on bank exposure to private-credit funds.

### 3. Strait of Hormuz: shipping effectively halted, not merely disrupted
**Fact**: IMF PortWatch data cited this week show only one transit through the Strait of Hormuz on 27 September, versus an 85-per-day pre-crisis baseline. At least six vessels were reported struck in the Strait in the week to ~3 October; a Kuwait Oil Tanker Co. vessel was hit by a projectile roughly five miles off Oman on 1 October, suffering a small fire and blackout before continuing under its own power. US officials have continued to describe the naval blockade response as sustainable indefinitely. Brent crude was trading around US$92/bbl on 6 October — down from the $100+ levels reported two weeks earlier.
**Analysis**: "near-total closure of the Strait to commercial shipping" is a materially different, more severe fact than "ongoing disruption," the framing used in the prior two reports. It is consistent with a structural shift toward non-Hormuz routing (the Saudi pipeline workaround flagged previously) rather than genuine de-escalation.
**Uncertainty**: the combination of near-zero Hormuz throughput and a Brent price below its late-September peak is not fully reconciled by available reporting — this report does not take a view on which side of that tension (sustained non-Hormuz supply vs. demand-side softening) dominates.

### 4. Northern Star: new CEO starts as the Gold Fields takeover remains unresolved

### 6. Fourth-hike mortgage pass-through completes amid a deepening housing correction
**Fact**: Following the RBA's 29 September hike to 4.60%, CBA, Westpac, NAB and ANZ all confirmed they will raise variable home-loan rates by the full 25bp, effective for most customers from Friday 9 October 2026; Macquarie Bank and the Teachers Mutual group of brands are also passing the increase on in full, effective 15 October and 8 October respectively. Savings-rate increases lagged noticeably, with CBA, NAB and ANZ initially announcing no change to deposit products even as they raised borrowing rates, before NAB and ANZ later revised their savings offers. Separately, Canstar data show the number of lenders offering any fixed rate below 6% has fallen sharply since the start of 2026 as fixed-rate repricing broadens across the sector. In parallel, Cotality data show capital-city dwelling values down roughly 5% from their April 2026 peak on its daily index, with the September quarter recording a 4.3% decline and the cumulative peak-to-date fall revised to 6.4% — within three percentage points of the largest downturn in the 40-year record (8.6%, October 2017–May 2019).
**Analysis**: banks are again repricing loans faster than deposits to protect margins, while the housing correction is now tracking toward record territory in tandem with, not despite, the RBA's four 2026 hikes — a genuinely new severity threshold relative to prior weeks' reporting, and directly relevant to the RBA Financial Stability Review's (#2) claim that most mortgaged households retain adequate buffers.
**Watch next**: October Cotality data for signs of further acceleration, and whether mortgage-arrears data (not yet due) begin to diverge from the RBA's resilience assessment.

## Emerging Trends

- **Private credit's stress test has moved from the sector's fringes to its largest listed players.** Bathla (a borrower collapse) and Remara (a mis-sold retail product) were weaknesses at the edges of the private-credit market; Metrics Credit Partners is a flagship, ASX-listed manager freezing funds on its own auditor's objection. Whether this is contained to one manager's governance practices or signals a broader valuation-discipline problem across the sector is the single most important open question for Australian credit markets into Q4.
- **A visible gap between the RBA's system-level reassurance and fund-level reality.** The RBA's Financial Stability Review called non-bank lender risk "contained by size" days before the largest private-credit freeze of the year — a reminder that system-level resilience claims do not preclude individual-manager shocks with reputational and confidence effects beyond their formal size.
- **The Hormuz risk regime has changed in kind, not just degree** — from disruption to near-total closure by transit-count — even as spot oil prices have not moved proportionally, an unresolved puzzle worth tracking into the RBA's November decision.
- **AI infrastructure capital formation continues even as the RBA flags its own financing opacity as a risk.** Firmus Grid's institutional bookbuild landed in the same week the RBA's FSR warned that AI-related long-term debt financing is "becoming more opaque and more vulnerable to profits falling short of expectations" — a direct tension between capital-markets enthusiasm and the central bank's own risk assessment.
- **The rate cycle and the housing correction are now moving together, not offsetting each other.** Mortgage pass-through completing in the same week Cotality data show the correction nearing record depth is a genuinely new severity signal relative to prior weeks.
- **A leadership-transition cluster** is underway simultaneously at Northern Star (new CEO, incoming chair) and Macquarie Group (CEO handover in four weeks) — companies this report has tracked closely for unrelated reasons are mid-transition at once.
