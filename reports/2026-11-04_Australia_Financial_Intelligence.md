## Executive Summary

1. **The RBA hiked the cash rate 25bp to 4.85% on 3 November — its fifth hike of 2026 and the highest cash rate since 2008 — in a split 6-3 board vote**, the first non-unanimous RBA decision of this hiking cycle. This confirms the hike risk this project's 28 October report flagged after a hot September-quarter CPI print, and the split vote itself is new information: three board members wanted to hold at 4.60%, a level of visible internal disagreement not present in September's unanimous 9-0 move.
2. **Westpac's FY26 result (released 2 November, the day before the RBA decision) showed the bank's margin squeezed and bad-debt charges rising just as the rate cycle re-accelerated**: statutory net profit of $6.8bn (down ~1%), pre-provision profit up 4% to $10.9bn, but credit impairment charges rising materially — to $864m from $424m a year earlier, per Kalkine's reporting of the result — and management explicitly guiding to interest rates staying elevated into 2027, directly corroborating the RBA's own tightening bias from the demand side.
3. **The US Federal Reserve held its target range at 3.75–4.00% on 28 October in a 10-2 vote, with Governors Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack dissenting in favour of a hike** — a genuinely hawkish-leaning hold that, combined with the RBA's move two trading days later, widens the AU-US policy-rate divergence (RBA 4.85% vs. Fed 3.75–4.00%) at a moment this report could not confirm the AUD/USD market reaction to either event.
4. **A correction to last week's Austal USA framing**: rather than a weaker rival bidder possibly stepping back, Wildcat Infrastructure's non-binding offer for Austal USA is a live, higher bid — US$1.25–1.35bn enterprise value, against Hanwha Defense USA's US$1.05–1.2bn — with Austal granting Wildcat four weeks of due diligence. ## Top Developments

### 1. RBA hikes to 4.85% in a split 6-3 vote — fifth hike of 2026, highest rate since 2008
**Fact**: at its 3 November 2026 meeting, the RBA's Monetary Policy Board raised the cash rate target by 25 basis points to 4.85%. The vote was split: six members voted for the increase, three voted to hold at 4.60%. This is the fifth cash-rate increase of 2026 and takes the rate to its highest level since 2008. The RBA's November Statement on Monetary Policy revised inflation forecasts higher, now seeing headline inflation peaking at roughly 4.5% in the December-2026 quarter before easing, with inflation expected to stay above 3% over the coming year. The Board's accompanying statement cited elevated and "broadly based" inflation, the war in the Middle East, and rising oil prices as reasons for the move, and — notably — dropped the explicit "will raise the cash rate further if needed" language that had appeared in the September statement. The next decision is scheduled for 8 December 2026.
**Analysis**: this confirms, in the most direct way possible, the hike risk this project's own 28 October report flagged after the hot September-quarter CPI print — the RBA followed through on the data rather than repeating the "wait and see" pattern of its late-2026 cycle so far. The 6-3 split is itself a materially new fact: every other 2026 hike on this project's record (February, March, May, September) has been described in available sources as closer to unanimous or with narrower dissent, so three members openly preferring a hold signals a genuine internal disagreement about how much further tightening is warranted, not just a hawkish consensus executing the obvious next step.
**Inference**: the removal of the explicit "will raise further if needed" guidance, combined with the 6-3 split, suggests the Board itself sees December as a more genuinely contestable decision than markets are currently pricing; several outlets reported this is raising talk of a December pause, but this report treats that reading as market/analyst inference, not a Board commitment.
**Uncertainty**: this report could not obtain a reliably-dated, confirmed AUD/USD reaction or ASX 200 closing level directly attributable to the decision from independently cross-checked sources (see Markets section) and does not state either as fact; some secondary outlets (Kalkine, IBTimes Australia, Business Recorder) reported an ASX 200 close of roughly 8,886.80 (+0.34% on the day) with late-session gains attributed to the dovish tone of the statement, but this report flags that figure as secondary-sourced and not independently confirmed against a primary exchange data feed.
**Watch next**: the 8 December RBA decision; whether December CPI data (due before that meeting) supports or undermines the "peak at 4.5%" December-quarter forecast; any RBA commentary on the composition of the 6-3 split.

### 2. Westpac's FY26 result: margins compress and bad debts rise as the rate cycle re-accelerates
**Fact**: Westpac's full-year 2026 result (year to 30 September 2026), released 2 November, showed statutory net profit of $6.8bn, down roughly 1% on FY25; net profit excluding notable items was $7.0bn, broadly flat. Pre-provision profit rose 4% to $10.9bn. The final dividend was 77 cents per share fully franked, taking total FY26 dividends to 154 cents, up from 153 cents in FY25 (ex-dividend 5 November, payment 21 December). The CET1 capital ratio stood at approximately 12.5%, above the bank's 11.25% target. Loan growth was reported at around 5% over the year, deposit growth around 6%. Net interest margin fell roughly 7bp to around 1.87%. Management guided explicitly to interest rates remaining elevated into 2027. Credit impairment charges rose materially year-on-year — Kalkine's reporting of the result gives $864m for FY26 against $424m in FY25, roughly double — up from the roughly $443m level disclosed at the May 1H26 result, consistent with the charge building through the second half; this report notes the specific full-year figure is sourced to secondary coverage of Westpac's own results rather than independently retrieved from Westpac's results pack line-by-line, and flags it accordingly as well-sourced but not primary-document-verified.
**Analysis**: this is the first big-four read on how margin compression and credit quality are behaving just as the RBA re-accelerated its tightening cycle one trading day later — pre-provision profit growth (+4%) alongside roughly flat bottom-line profit, with impairment charges roughly doubling, is a materially different story from the NIM-driven mortgage-competition narrative that dominated Westpac's reporting in August 2026 (see this project's 16 August report), and is the first hard, primary-institution-sourced confirmation — rather than a single-sourced and since-corrected NAB claim, or sell-side commentary alone — that the housing-correction/credit-quality theme tracked since September is showing up directly in a major bank's own numbers.
**Inference**: management's explicit "rates elevated into 2027" guidance, delivered the day before the RBA's own hike, suggests Westpac's own economists were already leaning toward the November-hike outcome that materialised — a signal that the big banks' internal rate calls are converging with, rather than lagging, the RBA's own reaction function this cycle. Whether the impairment rise is sector-wide or Westpac-specific is not yet established.
**Watch next**: NAB's FY26 result (5 November, outside this report's window) and ANZ's FY26 result for whether the margin/impairment pattern is sector-wide or Westpac-specific; any post-result analyst commentary on whether rising impairments reflect broad-based credit stress or idiosyncratic Westpac exposures.

### 3. US Fed holds at 3.75–4.00% in a hawkish-leaning 10-2 vote
**Fact**: the FOMC held its target range at 3.75–4.00% on 28 October 2026, by a 10-2 vote, with Governors Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack dissenting in favour of a 25bp hike. This followed the Fed's September hike and is described by several outlets as a "hawkish pause" — officials chose to wait for more inflation data while delivering a stronger assessment of current economic conditions. The next FOMC decision is 9 December 2026; multiple analysts characterise market pricing as leaning toward a December hike, though this is market-pricing inference, not a Fed commitment, and this report does not independently verify a specific probability figure.
**Analysis**: a hold with two hawkish dissents, landing two trading days before the RBA's own hike to 4.85%, widens the headline AU-US policy-rate gap to roughly 85-110bp (4.85% vs. 3.75-4.00%) — a material divergence that would typically be expected to support AUD/USD, all else equal.
**Uncertainty**: this report could not obtain a confirmed AUD/USD level for the period immediately following either central-bank decision from independently cross-checked sources, and does not state a figure as fact (see Markets section).

### 4. Austal USA: correcting last week's framing — Wildcat's bid is live and higher than Hanwha's, not a bidder stepping back
**Fact**: Wildcat Infrastructure, a Miami-based investment firm, has a non-binding indication of interest for Austal USA valuing the business at an aggregate enterprise value of US$1.25–1.35bn on a cash-free, debt-free basis, conditional on four weeks of due diligence — topping Hanwha Defense USA's earlier non-binding proposal of US$1.05–1.2bn. Hanwha (which already holds a 19.9% stake in Austal) remains separately engaged in its own due diligence process. Wildcat has told Austal it intends to retain the Austal brand and operate the US business as a standalone platform. Austal's board and advisers are evaluating Wildcat's proposal; no preferred bidder has been announced, and this report found no binding offer or resolution dated within the 29 October–4 November window from either side.
**Analysis**: this corrects, rather than confirms, last week's report's framing — which relied on Korean-press signals suggesting a rival bidder (plausibly Wildcat) might be stepping back from the Austal contest via a separate shipyard acquisition. This week's direct research instead finds Wildcat's own bid for Austal USA is real, live, and priced higher than Hanwha's — the two claims are not necessarily contradictory (a firm can pursue one acquisition while bidding on another), but this report could not re-find or corroborate last week's specific "rival bidder stepping back" claim this week, and flags it as unresolved rather than repeating it as settled.
**Inference**: a genuine two-horse bidding contest, with the higher bid coming from a US-based buyer that would avoid the foreign-ownership/CFIUS complications a Korean acquirer faces, plausibly strengthens Austal's negotiating position and lengthens the timeline before any deal closes.
**Watch next**: any binding offer from either Hanwha or Wildcat; confirmation of CFIUS involvement if Hanwha's bid advances; further Korean-press coverage of Hanwha's response to a higher competing offer.
