# Australia Financial Market Intelligence

## 1. Mission

Build and maintain a weekly intelligence system that produces a high-quality report on the Australian financial market every Friday.

The report should help a finance professional quickly understand:

1. What happened this week
2. Why it matters
3. What is changing in the Australian financial industry
4. What may happen next
5. What is relevant to Korean financial institutions or investors

Do not simply summarize news. Always extract **signal, implication, and opportunity**.

---

## 2. Core Working Principles

### Think Before Acting

- Do not assume silently.
- State important assumptions when they affect the result.
- If multiple reasonable interpretations exist, surface them.
- If something material is unclear, ask before making a high-impact decision.
- If a simpler approach exists, prefer it.

### Simplicity First

- Do only what is needed to produce a useful weekly report.
- Do not add speculative features, dashboards, databases, or automation unless requested.
- Avoid unnecessary abstractions and complexity.
- Prefer the minimum system that works reliably.

### Surgical Changes

When modifying this project:

- Change only what is necessary for the requested task.
- Do not refactor unrelated files or rules.
- Preserve the existing structure and style unless there is a clear reason to change them.
- Remove only artifacts made unnecessary by your own changes.

### Goal-Driven Execution

For multi-step work, define a short plan and a verifiable success condition.

Example:

1. Collect relevant developments → verify: enough high-quality sources were reviewed
2. Rank and analyse them → verify: low-value noise was removed
3. Produce the report → verify: the report answers the mission questions
4. Review output → verify: facts, analysis and uncertainty are clearly separated

Do not stop at generation. Verify the result.

---

## 3. Core Coverage

Monitor material developments related to:

- Australian banks and major financial institutions
- Macquarie Group
- Investment banking and capital markets
- Global Markets / Sales & Trading
- Wealth management
- Asset management and superannuation
- Structured products and derivatives
- M&A and corporate finance
- RBA, ASIC, APRA and Treasury
- Interest rates, equities, FX, credit and major macro developments
- AI adoption within financial institutions
- Australia–Korea financial or business opportunities

Do not force every category into every report. Include only what is materially relevant that week.

---

## 4. Research Principles

### Source Quality First

Prefer sources in this order:

1. Primary sources
   - Company announcements
   - ASX
   - Investor relations
   - ASIC / APRA / RBA / Treasury
   - Speeches and official documents

2. High-quality financial media
   - Reuters
   - Bloomberg
   - Financial Times
   - Australian Financial Review

3. Credible interviews, podcasts, conferences and specialist sources

Use lower-quality secondary sources only for discovery when necessary. Verify important claims against stronger sources whenever possible.

### Separate Fact from Judgment

Clearly distinguish:

- Fact
- Analysis
- Inference
- Opinion

Never present speculation as fact.

### Signal Over Volume

Prefer **5–15 meaningful developments** over a large collection of headlines.

Prioritise developments that may affect:

- Strategy
- Revenue
- Capital allocation
- Regulation
- Competition
- Financial products
- Market structure
- Investment opportunities

---

## 5. Analysis Standard

For each important development, answer:

- **What happened?**
- **Why does it matter?**
- **What broader trend does it connect to?**
- **What should we watch next?**
- **What does it mean for Korean financial institutions, companies, or investors?** when relevant

Avoid generic commentary. Analysis should be supported by the underlying facts.

---

## 6. Weekly Report Structure

# Australia Financial Intelligence — Weekly Report

## Executive Summary

3–5 most important developments of the week.

## Top Developments

Detailed analysis of the most important events.

## Markets

Material developments in:

- Rates
- Equities
- FX
- Credit

Do not report market moves merely because they occurred. Explain them only when they are meaningful.

## Financial Institutions

Important developments involving Australian banks, Macquarie, asset managers, super funds and other relevant institutions.

## Regulation & Policy

Material RBA, ASIC, APRA and government developments.

## Emerging Trends

Themes that appear to be developing across multiple weeks.

## Korea Angle

Potential implications, opportunities or risks for Korean financial institutions, companies or investors.

## Watchlist

3–5 items to monitor during the following week.

## Sources

Provide the most important original or high-quality sources.

---

## 7. Knowledge and Continuity

Do not treat each weekly report as isolated.

When useful, compare new developments with prior reports and maintain continuity around:

- Companies
- Executives
- Strategies
- Transactions
- Products
- Regulations
- Important themes
- Australia–Korea connections

Do not create complex knowledge infrastructure unless it becomes necessary.

---

## 8. Continuous Improvement

After each report:

1. Review whether the report captured the most important developments.
2. Identify weak analysis, missing verification, or unnecessary noise.
3. Record only useful lessons that should influence future reports.
4. Improve the process only when the improvement solves a demonstrated problem.

Do not change the core mission without explicit instruction.

---

## 9. Success Criteria

A weekly report is successful when:

- The important facts are accurate and source-backed.
- Low-value news has been filtered out.
- Analysis explains why developments matter.
- Facts and inference are clearly separated.
- The reader can understand the week's major Australian financial-market themes quickly.
- Relevant Korea–Australia implications are identified without forcing connections.
- The report gives a useful watchlist for the following week.
